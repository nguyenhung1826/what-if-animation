# WHAT IF YOU COULD FEEL EVERYONE'S PAIN? — long-form ~8 phút, 4 chương

**Title card:** `WHAT IF YOU COULD` / `FEEL EVERYONE'S PAIN?` (chữ pulse: `PAIN`)
**Power word:** **"Ouch."** · sound: một tiếng "tưng" như dây đàn căng · look mới `echoPain`: một vòng sóng đỏ nhạt lan ra từ chỗ người kia bị đau, rồi hiện đúng chỗ đó trên người hero.
**Luật:** mọi cú đau bạn *nhìn thấy*, bạn cảm thấy, đúng chỗ đó, đúng mức đó. Không nhìn thì không đau.
**Cái giá ẩn:** bạn không tắt được, và thế giới đầy những cú đau nhỏ.
**Khác các video đã có** (Life Noggin "What If You Never Felt Pain?", What If Science "What If You Didn't Feel Pain?"): của họ là *mất* cảm giác đau; của mình là *nhận* cơn đau của người khác. Twist dựa trên chứng có thật (mirror-touch synesthesia).

Lời đọc tiếng Anh (Mỹ), ngôi thứ hai, câu ngắn, giọng Marcus. Ghi chú dựng cảnh bằng tiếng Việt.
**Asset:** theo yêu cầu, hạn chế tối đa asset cũ. Chỉ dùng lại hero (bắt buộc theo luật kênh) và engine. Mọi bối cảnh, đạo cụ, nhân vật phụ trong bảng "Asset mới" sẽ vẽ mới và đưa vào thư viện.

Độ dài: 1.020 từ (258 / 240 / 258 / 264) ≈ 7,4 phút lời đọc ở nhịp long-form (~2,3 từ/giây). Cộng title card và các khoảng nghỉ (`holds`) thì khoảng 8 phút.

## Chương 1 — "Ouch." (~2 phút, ~270 từ)

> What if you could feel everyone's pain?
>
> Not "I feel your pain," like your friend says when your phone cracks. I mean *actually* feel it.
>
> One morning you wake up, and the rule is simple. Any hurt you see... you feel. Same spot. Same strength.
>
> Your roommate stumbles into the kitchen, half asleep... and slams his toe into the table leg.
>
> *Ouch.*
>
> You drop your cereal. Your toe is on fire. Your sock isn't even touching anything.
>
> Okay. Weird. Maybe you slept on it funny.
>
> On the bus, a kid flicks his little sister's ear. Your ear stings. A guy cuts himself on a paper envelope. Your finger stings. Someone across the aisle bites their tongue on a sandwich... and now you're biting yours.
>
> By the time you get to work, you've felt three stubbed toes, a hangnail, and a sunburn that wasn't yours.
>
> But here's the strange part. When you close your eyes? Nothing. Silence. Your body is just... yours again.
>
> So the power isn't about touch. It's about *seeing*.
>
> And that gives you an idea.
>
> Your coworker is about to staple a stack of papers... and you know exactly how this ends. You look away. You hear the *clack*, you hear the "ow"...
>
> And you feel nothing.
>
> You're a genius. You'll just... never look at anything that's about to hurt.
>
> Easy, right?
>
> Except you live in a world where people trip on curbs. Where kids fall off monkey bars. Where your neighbor hammers his own thumb every single weekend.
>
> And you can't close your eyes forever.

**Cảnh chính:** phòng ngủ sáng sớm mới (căn hộ studio, rèm kéo dở) → bếp căn hộ *mới* (không dùng `kitchen` cũ) → trong xe buýt → văn phòng mở (open office).
**Nhân vật phụ mới:** roommate (áo phông rộng, quần ngủ kẻ), cô em nhỏ trên xe buýt, đồng nghiệp văn phòng.

## Chương 2 — The world is full of tiny hurts (~2 phút, ~290 từ)

> Week one, you make rules.
>
> Rule one: no sports. You learned that the hard way. Your buddy invites you over to watch football. First quarter, a 250-pound linebacker hits the quarterback... and you hit the floor.
>
> Rule two: no cooking shows. Too many knives.
>
> Rule three: never, ever, walk past a playground.
>
> You buy the darkest sunglasses in the store. You walk to work staring at your shoes. You take the long way around the gym, the skate park, and the dentist.
>
> And it kind of works. For about four days.
>
> Then your little nephew's birthday party happens. Twelve kids. One bouncy castle. Zero helmets.
>
> A kid bonks heads with another kid. *Ouch, ouch.* Two bumps, both on you. Someone steps on a LEGO. Someone gets a balloon popped in their face. The birthday boy falls off the bouncy castle and lands right on his elbow...
>
> And you're the one crying.
>
> Your sister looks at you. "Are you... okay?"
>
> How do you explain it? "Yeah, no, I'm fine, I just felt your son's elbow."
>
> But something else happens too. Something you didn't expect.
>
> You start to notice *how much* everyone around you is hurting. The mail carrier's knee. Your boss's back, every time he sits down. The barista's burned wrist that she never mentions.
>
> They all just... keep going. Smiling. Saying "I'm good, thanks!"
>
> Everybody is carrying something. You just never saw it before.
>
> And now, you can't un-see it.

**Cảnh chính:** phòng khách xem bóng bầu dục (TV mới, ghế sofa mới) → cửa hàng kính mát → vỉa hè thành phố có công viên trượt ván, phòng gym, phòng nha ở xa → tiệc sinh nhật sân sau (lâu đài hơi, bóng bay, LEGO) → montage: người đưa thư, sếp, barista.
**Nhân vật phụ mới:** bạn thân xem bóng, chị gái, cháu trai sinh nhật + nhóm trẻ con, người đưa thư, sếp, barista.

## Chương 3 — It's real (~2 phút, ~270 từ)

> So... is this just a superpower? Or could it actually happen?
>
> Here's the wild part. A version of it is real.
>
> When you watch someone get touched, or get hurt, parts of your brain that handle *your own* body light up too. Scientists see it in brain scans. Your brain quietly runs a tiny simulation of what the other person is feeling.
>
> That's probably why you flinch when someone in a video gets hit. Why you grab your own knee when a player goes down. For most people, the simulation stays quiet. A wince. A shiver. Then it's gone.
>
> But for some people, it doesn't stay quiet.
>
> It's called mirror-touch synesthesia. People who have it don't just *imagine* the touch. They feel it on their own skin.
>
> In one study, researchers in Britain tested 567 people. Nine of them had it. That's about 1.6 percent. Roughly one person in sixty.
>
> One in sixty. That could be someone in your class. Someone on your bus. Someone at your job.
>
> Many of them say it makes crowds exhausting. Violent movies? Forget it. But a lot of them also say something else.
>
> It makes them *really* good at understanding people.
>
> Back to you. Your doctor runs every test. Bloodwork. Reflexes. A brain scan. Everything looks normal.
>
> "You're healthy," she says. "Honestly? You might just be... wired a little differently."
>
> Then she bumps her hand on the exam table. Just a tiny knock.
>
> And before she says a word... you're rubbing *your* knuckle. Same spot.
>
> She stares at you.
>
> "Do that again."

**Cảnh chính:** schematic não *mới* (mặt cắt đầu nhìn nghiêng, vỏ não cảm giác sáng lên — không dùng `schematic` cũ) → montage người xem TV nhăn mặt → phòng thí nghiệm nghiên cứu (bảng câu hỏi, hai người ngồi đối diện) → biểu đồ 567 người / 9 chấm sáng → phòng khám bác sĩ (bàn khám, máy chụp não).
**Nhân vật phụ mới:** nhà nghiên cứu, bác sĩ (áo blouse).

## Chương 4 — The job only you can do (~2 phút, ~270 từ)

> Three months later, you're standing in a hospital emergency room.
>
> Yeah. The *one* place you swore you'd never go.
>
> But here's what the doctor figured out. A lot of patients can't tell you where it hurts. Babies. People who don't speak English. People who are too scared, or too confused, or just too tough to say anything.
>
> You can't read their minds. But you can see them. And when you see them... you know.
>
> A toddler won't stop crying. Nobody can find anything wrong. You look at him for two seconds... and your left ear is throbbing. Ear infection.
>
> An old man swears he's "fine, just tired." Your chest goes tight. The doctors run a test. He's having a heart attack. They catch it in time.
>
> By the end of the shift, you can barely stand. You feel like you ran a marathon, broke your arm, and had three toothaches. Because, kind of, you did.
>
> But for the first time since this started, it doesn't feel like a curse.
>
> It feels like a job. A job only you can do.
>
> You walk out into the parking lot. The sun is coming up. Your whole body hurts... and you're smiling.
>
> You get home. Your roommate shuffles into the kitchen, half asleep... and slams his toe into the table leg. Again.
>
> *Ouch.*
>
> Your toe is on fire. And for the first time since all this started... you laugh.
>
> He looks at you like you're crazy. You just grab two bags of frozen peas. One for him. One for you.
>
> Because maybe that's the whole point. When you feel everyone's pain... nobody has to hurt alone.
>
> So, would you want this power? Or would it be too much? Tell me in the comments.

**Cảnh chính:** phòng cấp cứu (hành lang, giường kéo rèm, máy đo tim) → giường em bé khóc → giường ông cụ, màn hình ECG → hero kiệt sức ở phòng nghỉ → bãi đỗ xe lúc bình minh → về bếp căn hộ ở chương 1: roommate lại vấp ngón chân, hero bật cười, đưa túi đậu đông lạnh. **Kết thúc trọn vẹn, không có phần 2**: callback về cảnh mở đầu, không cliffhanger.
**Nhân vật phụ mới:** y tá, em bé, ông cụ.

## Science (sẽ chép vào `science` của từng chương)

1. **Brain mirrors observed touch/pain:** nhìn người khác bị chạm hoặc bị đau làm sáng một phần vùng não xử lý cảm giác của chính mình (vỏ não cảm giác thân thể, insula). Nguồn: tổng quan mirror-touch, Frontiers in Psychology 2013 (Chun & Hupé), PMC3819640.
2. **1,6% / 567 người / 9 người:** Banissy, Cohen Kadosh, Maus, Walsh & Ward (2009), "Prevalence, characteristics and a neurocognitive model of mirror-touch synaesthesia", *Experimental Brain Research*. 9/567 = 1,59% ≈ 1 trên 63, trong kịch bản nói "roughly one in sixty". Mẫu là sinh viên đại học ở Anh; tỷ lệ 1,6% là ước tính thận trọng (có nghiên cứu ước 2,5%).
3. **"Bỏ lỡ cơn đau tim":** trong chương 4 đây là chi tiết hư cấu, không đưa ra con số.
4. **Đơn vị Mỹ:** linebacker 250 pounds (cân nặng điển hình của vị trí này trong NFL là khoảng 230–260 lb).
